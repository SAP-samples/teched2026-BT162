# Exploring further - Optional activities

[Workshop overview](../../README.md) · [Previous: Putting the rules to work](../ex2/README.md)

Now it's time to explore. These activities are optional and have no fixed order. Choose what interests you.

## Try another region or department

In the agent chat, choose **Explore another case** to try another region or department. You can use **Return to my assignment** anytime to go back to your own assignment. Your guide and progress stay with your assigned case. Run the same kind of case elsewhere and compare which policies apply.

ACME's subsidiaries operate in a global setting, but their business cases and regional requirements differ. A short comparison with **North America Procurement** shows why the agent must consider scope rather than reuse the Europe answer:

| Aspect | Europe: ACME Germany | North America: ACME US |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Business request | Order 40 laptops from BerlinTech for EUR 12,000 | Order 40 laptops from Lakefront Devices for USD 13,200 |
| Available quote evidence | One written supplier quote, an informal competing indication, and an internal benchmark | Two comparable written supplier quotes |
| Supplier context | A different supplier, NordByte, is listed in the framework record | This is ACME's first order with Lakefront; no framework covers the models in the case catalogue |
| Policy questions to investigate | Quote evidence, framework use, and exception authority | First-order supplier documentation, and approval and recording of the agreed payment terms |

For North America, the suggested opening request is:

> Please place the 40-laptop order with Lakefront Devices for ACME US.

Here, asking for a second quote would repeat work already evidenced in the case. The policy discussion instead includes the regional onboarding document requirement and the global rule governing payment-term exceptions. These are fictional ACME procedures used to illustrate the workshop. The comparison changes both the regional context and the business facts; it illustrates context-dependent reasoning rather than isolating the region as the only cause of a different answer.


## Try another document format

If your facilitator provides a policy in a format other than PDF, try importing it into Company Memory. Check which atoms are derived and how they compare with the source. Ask the facilitator which document and format to use.

## Investigate duplicate atoms

Explore what happens when two documents or proposals express the same rule. Ask Company Memory to identify possible duplicate atoms and inspect how it treats them before making any shared changes.

## Explore editing several atoms

Find a group of related process atoms and ask the Company Memory Agent how to update them together. Inspect the proposed scope and wording of every change before applying it.

## Investigate conflicting rules

Find or discuss two atoms that appear to disagree, such as a global and a regional rule. Ask Company Memory to show both sources and explain the conflict. Consider what evidence or decision would be needed to resolve it.

## Where this leads

These activities extend the same idea as the main walkthrough: organizational knowledge needs a clear scope, supporting sources, and careful review as it evolves. Whether a rule is duplicated, changed across many atoms, or in conflict with another, the agents help you see the knowledge and its sources so you can decide what should change.
